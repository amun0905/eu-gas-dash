Weather tab: how the numbers are made

Country temperatures

Each country's temperature is a population-weighted average: where more people live counts for more, because that's where gas is burnt for heating. It is built in four steps, the same for every country:

  1. Population from the EU's GHS-POP grid (2025, ~1 km cells) is added up on ECMWF's 0.25° forecast grid, counting only cells inside the country's border.
  2. A few sample points are picked per country: the most populated grid point first, then the next most populated one at least D km from every point already picked, where D = 0.6 × √(country area ÷ number of points). This spreads points across the country instead of piling them into the capital.
  3. Every populated grid point is assigned to its nearest sample point. A point's weight is the population it represents ÷ the country's population, so the weights add up to 100% of residents, not just city centres.
  4. Each day, ECMWF's daily mean 2 m temperature (UTC day) at each sample point is multiplied by its weight and summed.

The points and weights are listed below. They are generated by scripts/build_weather_points.py from the open datasets above, so anyone can rerun it and get the same numbers. Stored data is tagged with the version of the points that built it, so a change to the points never mixes old and new weights.

Normal

The average of the last 10 complete years (currently 2016–2025) of ERA5 for each calendar day, using the same points and weights, then smoothed with a centred 15-day average. "vs normal" = temperature − normal. The same 10-year normal is used for heating degree days on the Storage tab.

Forecasts

Past days: ERA5 reanalysis (about 5 days behind real time); the most recent few days come from ECMWF's short-range forecast until ERA5 catches up. ECMWF HRES: the single high-resolution (~9 km) forecast, out to about 15 days. ENS mean: average of the 51 ensemble members (0.25°); each member is averaged over the country first, and the shaded band is the 10th–90th percentile of those 51 country averages. vs previous run: latest 00z or 12z run minus the one before; 06z and 18z runs are stored but not compared because they only reach about 6 days ahead.

Limitations

  • Population is a proxy for heating demand, not demand itself: industry, gas-fired power and differences in how homes are heated aren't captured.
  • Each country uses 3–6 sample points (51 in total, a limit set by the free data service), so a point stands in for a wide area around it. Commercial providers typically average over every grid cell.
  • Days are UTC calendar days, not gas days (06:00–06:00 CET).
  • The United Kingdom includes Northern Ireland, which isn't part of the GB gas market (about 3% of UK population).
  • Point forecasts are the model's nearest grid value, so a point near the coast or hills can differ a little from the city it's named after; for anomalies this largely cancels out because forecast and normal use the same points.
